How renting works, country by country

General pointers to help first-time tenants in each market — not legal advice. Rules change, so always confirm the current requirements with a local lawyer, the municipality, or your embassy before signing anything.

Kuwait

Rent is often paid by post-dated cheques covering the lease term, so check what your bank requires before signing. A security deposit and a copy of your Civil ID (for residents) are usually requested.

Bahrain

Tenancy disputes are handled through Bahrain's Rental Disputes Resolution process, so keep your signed contract and payment records. A deposit of around one month's rent is typical.

Oman

Tenancy contracts are commonly registered with the local municipality once signed. Have your passport or resident card ready, and clarify who pays municipality and utility fees before signing.

Jordan

A written lease is strongly recommended and sets out notice periods for renewal or termination, which can otherwise default to local tenancy law. Confirm whether the rent is agreed in Jordanian dinars or indexed to another currency.

Lebanon

Older and newer rental law regimes can apply very differently depending on when a tenancy began, so it's worth confirming which law governs your contract before signing. Settle the currency of payment in writing.

Turkey

Written tenancy contracts are standard, and Turkish law caps the deposit at up to three months' rent. You'll typically need a copy of your passport or residence permit, and contracts are often renewed annually.

Egypt

Egypt has both 'old law' and 'new law' rental regimes with different tenant protections, so check which applies to the property. A written contract and a copy of your ID or passport are usually expected.

Iraq

Practices vary by city and region, so a clear written contract and copies of ID for both parties are strongly recommended. Confirm deposit, currency and notice-period terms directly with the landlord before signing.
